# Code of Conduct

> **Sample policy for PolicyLens testing.** This is general example content,
> not an approved company policy or legal advice. An organization should review
> and adapt it before use.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This sample describes everyday standards for employees, temporary staff,
interns, contractors, and other people acting on behalf of an organization.
It applies during work, work travel, customer or vendor interactions, and
organization-sponsored events.

## 2. Professional behavior

People are expected to act honestly, respectfully, and in good faith. They
should communicate clearly, treat colleagues and customers fairly, follow
reasonable work instructions, protect confidential information, and use
organization resources for authorized purposes.

Threatening, abusive, discriminatory, or deliberately humiliating conduct is
not acceptable. Disagreements should be raised constructively and handled
without retaliation or personal attacks.

## 3. Accurate records and responsible decisions

Work, expense, time, purchasing, and customer records must be complete and
truthful. Employees must not falsify approvals, hide material facts, misuse
funds, or offer or accept improper benefits to influence a business decision.
Questions about a questionable request should be raised with a manager or the
appropriate internal team before proceeding.

## 4. Confidentiality and company property

Use company devices, accounts, documents, and data only for legitimate,
authorized work. Do not share confidential information with people who do not
need it for their role. Follow the organization's information security,
privacy, records-retention, and acceptable-use procedures.

## 5. Conflicts of interest

Personal, family, financial, or outside-work interests must not improperly
influence work decisions. Disclose a possible conflict promptly so it can be
reviewed. A disclosed conflict is not automatically misconduct; hiding or
misrepresenting one may be.

## 6. Raising a concern

Report suspected misconduct to a manager, Human Resources, Compliance, or an
available reporting channel. Share facts honestly and preserve relevant
records. The organization should review concerns fairly and limit information
to people who need it for the review. Retaliation against someone who raises a
concern in good faith or participates honestly in a review is prohibited.

## 7. Accountability

The organization should assess reported conduct consistently, consider the
available evidence, and apply appropriate corrective action under its
established procedures. Questions about how this sample applies should be
directed to the organization's designated policy owner.
