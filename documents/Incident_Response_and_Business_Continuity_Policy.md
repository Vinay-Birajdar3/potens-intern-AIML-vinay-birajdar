# Incident Response and Business Continuity

> **Sample policy for PolicyLens testing.** This is general example content,
> not an operational emergency plan. Use the organization's approved contacts,
> escalation steps, and recovery procedures.

## 1. What is an incident

An incident is an event that disrupts work, threatens people or facilities,
affects important systems or information, or may prevent the organization
from delivering a critical service. Examples include a suspected cyberattack,
data exposure, major system outage, utility interruption, severe weather, or
loss of an essential supplier.

## 2. Report and protect

Report suspected incidents promptly through the designated service desk,
security, facilities, safety, or emergency channel. If people may be in
immediate danger, contact local emergency services first and follow site
safety instructions.

Provide known facts: what happened, when it started, which service or location
is affected, how to contact you, and actions already taken. Do not speculate,
delete relevant evidence, or share incident details publicly.

## 3. Triage and response

The designated response lead assesses urgency, potential impact, and needed
specialists. Response teams may include Security, IT, Privacy, Legal,
Communications, Human Resources, Facilities, and business owners.

Authorized responders coordinate containment, investigation, recovery, and
communications. Personnel should follow their instructions and avoid
unapproved changes that could worsen the disruption or destroy evidence.

## 4. Business continuity

Teams should identify critical activities, key dependencies, alternate
contacts, and approved workarounds in their continuity plans. During a
disruption, managers should account for personnel, prioritize essential
services, communicate through approved channels, and record significant
decisions.

Use alternate sites, devices, systems, or suppliers only when authorized and
safe. Workarounds must continue to protect confidential information and
preserve required approvals and records.

## 5. Recovery and communications

The response lead confirms when systems or services can safely return to
normal operation. Restore from approved backups, validate critical functions,
and monitor for recurring issues. Only authorized spokespeople should make
external statements or notify customers, regulators, or affected individuals.

## 6. After-action review

After a significant incident, the responsible teams should document the
timeline, impact, decisions, recovery actions, and lessons learned. Assign
owners to corrective actions and update response and continuity plans.
Protect incident records according to security, privacy, and retention rules.
