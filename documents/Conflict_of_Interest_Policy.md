# Conflict of Interest

> **Sample policy for PolicyLens testing.** This is general example content,
> not approved legal, financial, or employment advice. Follow the
> organization's current disclosure and approval process.

## 1. What is a conflict

A conflict of interest may arise when a personal, family, financial, or
outside-work interest could affect—or reasonably appear to affect—someone's
work judgment. A potential conflict should be disclosed even if no improper
decision has occurred.

## 2. Common examples

Examples include selecting a vendor owned by a family member, supervising a
close personal relationship, using confidential company information for
personal gain, holding an undisclosed financial interest in a business
decision, or using work time or equipment for an outside business.

Outside employment, consulting, board service, investments, gifts, and
personal relationships are not automatically prohibited. Their relevance
depends on the person's role, access, decision-making authority, and applicable
organization rules.

## 3. Disclosure

Disclose an actual, potential, or perceived conflict promptly to a manager,
Human Resources, Compliance, or the designated ethics contact. Describe the
relationship or interest, related work decisions, and any relevant timing.
Update the disclosure if circumstances change.

Do not make or influence a related decision while waiting for guidance.
Managers should refer disclosures to the appropriate reviewer rather than
decide alone when they have an interest in the matter.

## 4. Review and management

The organization should assess the facts and choose proportionate safeguards.
Possible steps include additional approval, recusal from a decision,
reassignment of duties, restricted access, or documented approval to continue.
A disclosed conflict does not automatically mean wrongdoing.

## 5. Gifts and hospitality

Do not offer or accept anything intended to improperly influence a decision.
Disclose gifts or hospitality under the organization's current reporting
rules. Extra care is required in procurement, hiring, regulatory, and
customer decisions.

## 6. Records and non-retaliation

Keep disclosures and decisions in the approved restricted-access record
system. Raise concerns honestly. Retaliation against someone who discloses a
conflict or participates honestly in a review is prohibited.
